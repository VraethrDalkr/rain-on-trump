About "Is It Raining on Trump?"

What This Project Does

"Is It Raining on Trump?" estimates Donald J. Trump's current location from public data (aircraft tracking and his published schedule from Factba.se), then asks a weather service whether it is raining or snowing there. The answer is a big YES or NO over a sky that matches the weather, with the rain and snow rates. Tap the location card to see where the location comes from, how sure we are, and the last places he was.

Whether a rainy notification brightens your day or prompts you to send thoughts and prayers, we don't judge.

Data Sources & Methodology

1. How the location is chosen. Each source gives reports of the form "he was at this place at this time", and each kind of report has its own level of trust. The app uses the newest trusted report. Weaker ones are used only when nothing better exists. A report stops counting once a newer trusted one puts him more than 60 km (about 37 miles) away.

2. Aircraft tracking. Community aircraft-tracking feeds (adsb.fi, with adsb.lol as backup) show the planes he flies on in the air, on the ground and when they land. Several aircraft can fly as Air Force One, so the app trusts a flight most when it is confirmed: the "AF1" callsign was seen, the schedule lists a matching departure or arrival, or it is the plane he was last known to use. A long flight that isn't confirmed counts a little less. Short local flights, such as training runs, are used only when nothing better is known.

3. Public schedule (Factba.se). An event with a time and a known venue is trusted; an item with only a date is not. On a long trip, a "departs X for Y" item means he is in the air until the matching arrival or the estimated arrival time. Items that don't say where he is are ignored: press "lids" (the end of the press day), cancelled events, and items for the First Lady or the Vice President alone.

4. Venue lookup. Common venues come from a hand-made list. Others are looked up on OpenStreetMap. When a name matches several places, the app drops any he could not have reached in time from where he was recently and picks the one closest to the rest of his schedule. So "Elysée Palace" means Paris during a trip to France.

5. Overnight. Between 9 PM and 8 AM Eastern, if his last evening event and his next morning event are both near the same one of Washington, Palm Beach or Bedminster, the app assumes he spent the night there: at the White House, Mar-a-Lago or his Bedminster golf club.

6. Confidence. The percentage is how much the app trusts the newest report. Each kind of report starts at its own level (82% for a timed schedule item, for example) and keeps it for 3 hours. After that it falls with age: about two thirds of the starting level after a day, under half after three days. At the White House, Mar-a-Lago or Bedminster it stops at half, since he tends to stay there. It never goes above 95%. Reports older than 7 days are dropped; if nothing usable is left the app shows ??? with the last place he was seen.

7. Weather. Open-Meteo data in 15-minute steps. It counts as raining (or snowing) if any of the last four steps, one hour in all, had measurable rain (or snow). So YES stays on until a full hour is dry, and the rate shown is the total for that hour. Thunderstorms come from the hourly weather code, which also tells when a storm brings hail.

8. Notifications. Push alerts when rain or snow starts or stops where he is, and when a thunderstorm starts, gets worse or passes. Settings has a switch for each of rain starting, rain stopping and thunderstorms. Storm alerts follow the rain switches: a storm starting or getting worse needs "Rain starts" on, a storm passing needs "Rain stops" on.

Limitations

The answer is a best guess from public information:

  • Location: depends on the public schedule and the aircraft feeds. The schedule can be late or wrong, and the plane isn't always visible.
  • Weather: forecast-model data, with no measurement at his exact spot. Very short showers can be missed, and the answer can run about 20 minutes behind (thunderstorms up to an hour).
  • In flight: while he is in the air the app answers NO without checking the weather, and checks again once he has landed.

Privacy

No cookies, no analytics, and the app never asks for your location. If you turn on notifications, the server keeps your browser's push address and which alerts you chose. Turning notifications off deletes it. The contact form above is sent through Formspree.
